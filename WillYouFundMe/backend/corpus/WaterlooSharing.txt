1. Government of Nunavut – Department of Community Services (CS) – previously known as Economic Development and Transportation (ED&T)
Main Territorial Partner and Core Funder for Hamlet CEDO positions
Community Capacity Building (CCB) Program – Core funding for the CEDO position and local community economic development projects. (no application required for this program as it is normally automatically funded) 

Department focus: Business development, community economic growth, infrastructure (transportation) support.
Key programs include:
Small Business Support Program — Supports start-up and growth of businesses in Nunavut. (Government of Nunavut)
Includes sub-funds such as “Small Business Opportunities Fund”, “Sustainable Livelihood Fund”. 
Strategic Investments Program (SIP) — For larger scale business or community economic development initiatives across industries. – High priority for CEDOs
Country Food Distribution Program (CFDP) – Provides funding to Nunavut communities and Hunters & Trappers Organizations to support country food harvesting, storage, and distribution through community freezers and harvesting infrastructure. – High priority for CEDOs
Community Tourism & Cultural Industries Program — Supports tourism, cultural industry and related infrastructure in communities. – High priority for CEDOs
Program Partnerships / Grants & Contributions Policy — The policy sets out the guiding Inuit societal values and eligibility criteria for the department’s grants. (Government of Nunavut)

Department of Culture and Heritage (C&H)
Department focus: Culture, heritage, languages (Inuktut and French), arts.
Key programs include:
Grants & Contributions for Culture, Language and Heritage — Supports non-profits, community-based organizations, individuals in promoting/preserving heritage, Indigenous language work, arts. (Government of Nunavut)
Example sub-streams: Heritage Facilities, Public Library Services, Language Implementation Fund, Private Sector Inuktut support. (Government of Nunavut)

Other relevant departmental programs
Department of Finance publishes the “Grants and Contributions 2023-24” report which gives visibility to funding across departments including CS, C&H, and C&GS. (Government of Nunavut)

2. Canadian Northern Economic Development Agency (CanNor) – See filed application EOI and Full application. Cannor first invites Expressions of Interest and then successful EOIs are invited to send a full application. 
More info here - 2026-2027 Expression of Interest
Primary federal funder for northern economic growth and innovation.
Key programs:
Northern Indigenous Economic Opportunities Program (NIEOP) – Supports Indigenous organizations and communities in developing business and economic opportunities.
Inclusive Diversification and Economic Advancement in the North (IDEANorth) – Focused on innovation, clean growth, and community infrastructure.
Regional Economic Growth through Innovation (REGI – Northern Component) – Funds projects that build innovation capacity and regional competitiveness.

3. Indigenous Services Canada (ISC)
Focus on community infrastructure, business development, and capacity building.
Key programs:
Community Opportunity Readiness Program (CORP) – Funds feasibility studies, business plans, and infrastructure to support community-owned businesses.

4. Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC)
Supports community and labour force development.
Key programs:
Skills and Partnership Fund (SPF) – Funds employment and training initiatives tied to community economic priorities.

5. Natural Resources Canada (NRCan)
For community energy, mining readiness, and clean technology.
Key programs:
Clean Energy for Rural and Remote Communities Program – Funds renewable energy feasibility and deployment projects.
Indigenous Natural Resource Partnerships Program – Supports Indigenous participation in resource development.

6. Infrastructure Canada
Supports large-scale community and regional infrastructure projects.
Key programs:
Investing in Canada Infrastructure Program (ICIP) – Streams for green, community, and rural infrastructure.
Green and Inclusive Community Buildings Program (GICB) – Supports energy-efficient and resilient community facilities.

7. Crown-Indigenous Relations and Northern Affairs Canada (CIRNAC)
Supports governance, land use, and regional planning.
Key programs:
Northern REACHE Program – Renewable energy capacity and efficiency in northern communities.
Climate Change Preparedness in the North (CCPN) – Funding for adaptation projects in northern municipalities.

9. Nunavut Development Corporation (NDC) & Nunavut Business Credit Corporation (NBCC)
Territorial corporations providing investment and lending support.
NDC: Equity investment in culturally and economically significant businesses.
NBCC: Loans and financial advisory services for small businesses and community enterprises.

10. Other Occasional Funders
BDC (Business Development Bank of Canada) – Loans and advisory services for small business.
Canadian Heritage – Cultural and heritage project funding.
Polar Knowledge Canada (POLAR) – Research and innovation support related to northern development.
CMHC (Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation) – For housing feasibility studies and community infrastructure.




















Directory of Funding Programs – Nunavut Regional Inuit Associations
Context: CEDOs often help citizens apply for the following funding programs, but rarely apply for them themselves, except the ones highlighted. 

1. Qikiqtani Inuit Association (QIA)
📍 Covers the Qikiqtaaluk (Baffin) region
🌐 www.qia.ca
Program
Description & Typical Use
Eligible Applicants
Qikiqtani Cultural Activities Program (QCAP)
Grants for on-the-land, cultural, and intergenerational learning activities (e.g., hunting camps, sewing, language workshops).
Individuals, groups, and organizations in Qikiqtani communities.
Ilagiiktunut Fund
Supports projects that strengthen family, community well-being, and cultural identity. Often funds local gatherings, youth leadership, and community wellness projects.
Inuit-led organizations, community groups, and individuals.
Community Hunt Program
Provides funding to support community-organized hunts and harvesting programs that share food with residents.
Hamlet councils and Hunters & Trappers Organizations (HTOs).
Harvesters Support Program (Equipment & Supplies)
Helps purchase boats, snowmobiles, and safety equipment to support harvesting livelihoods.
Individual Inuit harvesters and HTOs.
QIA Scholarships and Bursaries
Financial support for Inuit students pursuing post-secondary education or trades.
Inuit students enrolled in recognized programs.
QIA Training & Employment Initiatives
Funding for job-readiness and technical skills programs, often tied to major projects like Baffinland.
Inuit job seekers and training organizations.


2. Kitikmeot Inuit Association (KIA)
📍 Covers the Kitikmeot region (Cambridge Bay, Kugluktuk, etc.)
🌐 www.kitia.ca
Program
Description & Typical Use
Eligible Applicants
Inuit Initiatives Fund
Supports community-based programs that enhance culture, wellness, or recreation. Can cover community events, youth programs, or wellness activities.
Individuals, organizations, and hamlets.
Public Events Fund
Helps communities organize events that promote Inuit culture, traditional activities, and community wellness.
Community groups, schools, and hamlets.
Nunavut Sivummut Program
Small grants for Inuit-owned microbusinesses, artisans, or individuals starting local ventures.
Individual Inuit entrepreneurs and small business owners.
Harvesters Support Program / Freight Subsidy
Assists with costs of shipping and transporting harvesting goods and equipment.
Individual harvesters, HTOs.
Post-Secondary Scholarship Program
Provides financial assistance to Inuit students in post-secondary studies.
Inuit students from the Kitikmeot region.
Kitikmeot Mining Benefits Programs
Funded through Impact Benefit Agreements; supports employment, training, and business development related to regional mining projects.
Inuit-owned businesses and individuals.

4. Common Features Across All RIAs
All three associations share similar funding categories that CEDOs can help community members access:
Harvesting and On-the-Land Programs
Cultural and Language Revitalization Projects
Education and Training Scholarships
Community Wellness and Event Funding
Small Business and Entrepreneurial Grants
Applications typically require:
A short proposal or application form
Project description and community benefits
Budget and timeline
Proof of Inuit enrollment (Nunavut Tunngavik Inc. number)
